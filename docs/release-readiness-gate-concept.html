01 · The business case

QAPulse has the evidence. It needs the decision.

Requirements, test results, defects, risks, UAT, traceability, milestones and approvals already exist. Today, decision-makers still have to interpret those signals manually and may reach different conclusions from the same data.

1

Fragmented judgement

Each dashboard is locally useful, but no single control combines the evidence into a release decision.

2

Hidden policy

Readiness rules often live in meetings, spreadsheets or individual experience rather than an agreed policy.

3

Weak accountability

Exceptions may be discussed, but the rationale, owner, expiry and supporting evidence are difficult to audit later.

The opportunity: use existing QAPulse data to produce a consistent, explainable and formally approved readiness outcome—without creating another reporting workflow.
02 · The operating model

Evidence in. Policy applied. Decision out.

The gate should calculate readiness from authoritative records, preserve human accountability for exceptions, and show exactly why a release is blocked.

    07 · Delivery approach

    Deliver policy before complexity

    Phase 1 · 2 weeks

    Policy and evidence map

    1. Confirm release types and decision owners.
    2. Agree seven rules, thresholds and hard gates.
    3. Validate source-data completeness.
    4. Baseline existing decision lead time.
    Phase 2 · 3–4 weeks

    Read-only gate MVP

    1. Calculate gate status for one pilot project.
    2. Show evidence and failed-rule explanations.
    3. Generate a printable readiness pack.
    4. Do not allow exceptions yet.
    Phase 3 · 3–4 weeks

    Governed decisions

    1. Add exception request and approval.
    2. Capture rationale, evidence and expiry.
    3. Notify owners of failed gates.
    4. Compare pilot outcomes with baseline.
    08 · Risks and controls

    What could undermine the concept

    False confidenceA green result is only reliable if source data is complete. Display freshness and missing-data warnings; never silently treat missing evidence as passed.
    Gaming the thresholdsVersion policy changes, restrict who may edit them, and show which policy version governed each decision.
    Approval bottlenecksUse role-based delegation, due dates and escalation—not shared accounts or automatic approvals.
    Exception normalisationTrack repeat exceptions, require expiry dates and surface teams or gates with recurring waivers.
    One policy for everythingDefine profiles for production release, hotfix, pilot and internal deployment while keeping a small controlled set of variants.
    Recommended decision: approve a limited MVP for one live milestone. Begin with a read-only assessment using current QAPulse data, validate it against the existing release meeting, and add exception approval only after the team trusts the calculation.
